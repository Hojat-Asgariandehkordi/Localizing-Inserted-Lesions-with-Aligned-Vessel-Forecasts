\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether a vessel forecast helps a nodule candidate scorer because it is placed correctly or because it adds information. Paired crops with a shared exterior and a rotation sham answer it directly: on held-out data, alignment changes the location the scorer returns far more than the decision it makes, and a plain fixed subtraction is enough to realize this benefit. The same localization gain appears on scans with expert-annotated native vessels.

The study has clear limits. All positive targets are inserted signals, so real nodules, scan-level proposal pipelines, partial-volume effects and per-scan false-positive rates remain untested. The prespecified crop-level criterion was not met, the localization robustness checks were conducted post hoc, and absolute localized sensitivity remains modest. The most direct next step is a prespecified scan-level evaluation on adjudicated real nodules with localized sensitivity as the primary endpoint. A second is to evaluate, on localization, learned scorers that condition on an aligned forecast explicitly. A forecaster that follows curved vessels may help further on native anatomy. Placement shams offer a simple control for claims that spatial context helps.
